## What Happened

On the afternoon of 19 August 2026, during a casual conversation in the
CatShark TreeHouse chat, a member made a passing remark that touched on
ethnicity. Another member disagreed with it. The exchange got heated for a
few messages and then died down on its own within about ten minutes, after a
third member changed the subject.

About half an hour later the topic was brought up again in the chat, this
time by staff. Several admins joined in over the following minutes. The
conversation moved from the original remark to whether it had been
appropriate, a rule change on the subject was announced in the chat, and the
member who made the original remark said they no longer felt comfortable and
stepped away for a while.

The discussion continued afterwards in other channels and in private
messages between some of the people involved.

## Outcome

A disagreement that had already ended was reopened and became a longer, more
public discussion than it needed to be. Nobody was happy with how it went,
members included. The team reviewed the whole sequence the next day.

## Actions Taken

Following the review, the team took these actions.

- **Extended Rule 2 of the Network Rules** with § 2.5 and § 2.6:
  discrimination based on ethnicity is now explicitly prohibited, not only
  discrimination based on race, and grouping or judging anybody by their
  ethnic background is banned outright, whichever group is being talked
  about. Rule 2 also now states that an admin may ask you to drop a topic.
  See the [Network Rules](/rules/network-rules/#25-ethnicity).
- **Extended Rule 4 of the Network Rules** with § 4.4 and § 4.5: remain
  civil. Disagreement is not a license to tell somebody to shut up; explain
  yourself once or step away, and report it if it continues. See the
  [Network Rules](/rules/network-rules/#44-remain-civil).
- **Removed two members** from the network, and **temporarily banned a
  third** for one week. Each decision was based on the member's overall
  history with the network, not on this conversation alone. All three are
  listed in the [ban register](/moderation/bans/).
- **Reviewed how staff handled the situation**, and used that review to write the
  [Moderation Strategy](/moderation/strategy/).

## What We Learned

1. **A topic that has died should stay dead.** If it needs follow-up, that
   happens in private, not in the chat.
2. **Moderate conduct, not opinions.** Whether a remark was right or wrong is
   not a moderation question. How people treat each other is.
3. **One voice at a time.** When several staff respond to the same situation
   at once, the chat gets louder, not calmer. One person handles a live
   situation; everyone else stays out of the member chat.
4. **Private matters stay private.** Requests, warnings and anything about a
   specific member are handled in direct messages.
5. **Rules are not made mid-incident.** Changes to the rules go through the
   owners and are announced once they are decided, not during a heated
   moment.

## What Changes

These lessons are now written down as the
[Moderation Strategy](/moderation/strategy/), and the rule change is recorded
in the [Network Rules](/rules/network-rules/#amendment-log) under § 2 and § 4.
